# SignalScope — hackathon presentation script

**Format:** one presenter, about four minutes plus questions. Speak naturally; the words in quotation marks are lines to say, not text to read from the screen. Keep `/opportunities` open in a second tab and set the product to the fictional campus backpack before presenting.

## The pitch

### 0:00–0:35 — The hook [Home]

“Imagine you're launching a backpack. One customer says the straps broke. Another says, ‘I almost bought it, but I couldn't tell whether it could carry my laptop.’ A third loves that it's light and says, ‘Please don't make it heavier.’

If you count stars and keywords, you hear three disconnected comments. If you're the product team, you have a much harder question: **What should we change, for whom, and what would prove we were right?**

That's the question SignalScope helps answer.”

### 0:35–1:00 — Why a company cares [Home → Try now]

“Companies have feedback everywhere, but a review is only one person's experience. People who never bought the product often leave no review at all. And the loudest complaint isn't necessarily the best redesign brief.

SignalScope puts the customer statement, the purchase barrier, the opposing preference, and the proposed test into one decision trail. Let me show you.”

### 1:00–2:35 — Live decision story [/opportunities]

**Scene 1: Evidence arrives.** Click **Import fictional evidence**.

“Here are three fictional passages. The first owner reports a failure. The second is a non-buyer who chose something else because the load rating was unclear. The third likes the bag precisely because it's light. Notice that the non-buyer would disappear if we only read post-purchase reviews.”

**Scene 2: Need emerges.** Click **Next**.

“The common need is confidence carrying a heavy load. But the two people point to different causes: one describes a broken strap; one describes missing information. You can open the original passages right here. We don't turn two stories into a fake statistic.”

**Scene 3: Trade-off appears.** Click **Next**.

“Now the catch: stronger straps might add weight or cost, and this lighter-load customer doesn't want that. If we optimize for one segment without hearing the other, we could make a better bag for some people and a worse one for others.”

**Scene 4: Change is proposed.** Click **Next** and select the three proposal cards in turn.

“So we don't jump straight to ‘redesign the strap.’ We compare three moves: reinforce it, publish an honest load specification, or test a strap warranty. They address different reasons someone might walk away, and each has a different cost or quality risk.”

**Scene 5: Decision is tested.** Select **Reinforce the straps** and click **Next**.

“Here's the part I want you to remember. We don't stop at a recommendation. We say what we would test, what success looks like, and what result would make us change our mind. For reinforced straps, we'd compare the concept with today's bag across heavy-load and light-load customers, then measure both preference and actual failures. If failures don't improve, or added weight loses the intended customer, we reverse course. Actual sales lift would require a later controlled launch test.”

### 2:35–3:20 — Kearney fit [Open /kearney]

“For Kearney's PERLab work, this becomes a repeatable way to take consumer evidence into product renewal. The workspace lets us select a publicly documented offering, distinguish Kearney's published description from customer feedback, inspect sourced comparisons, identify gaps, and build a research roadmap. If evidence isn't available, it says so instead of inventing a competitor score or a customer opinion.

The backpack is our clearly labeled demo. The Kearney workspace is a public-source prototype; it is not a private Kearney integration or an analysis of a real client. With a client-approved product, customer corpus and cost data, this same decision pattern could be applied to an actual product family.”

**Stage direction:** Show **Select product → Analyze → Build roadmap** only if a report is already populated. If AI or source capture is unavailable, show the published offering and the empty-evidence state; do not wait for a live run.

### 3:20–4:00 — Close [Return to scene 5]

“The value isn't another dashboard telling a company that customers are unhappy. It's helping a product team make a decision they can defend: **here's the customer who needs it, here's the customer we might lose, here are the options, and here's the test that decides.**

Our next step is a small, permissioned pilot: one product family, a set of original customer passages including non-buyers, a real cost and margin constraint, and an owner who can run the test. Then we measure whether this process helps the team reach a better decision faster. That's SignalScope: hear the need, shape what's next.”

## If you have only 90 seconds

“A broken strap review says ‘make it stronger.’ A non-buyer says ‘I couldn't find the load rating.’ Another customer says ‘don't make my bag heavier.’ Which one do you listen to?

SignalScope turns these competing signals into a testable product decision. [Show `/opportunities` scenes 1, 2, 3.] We keep each claim linked to its original passage and include the customer who didn't buy. [Show scene 4.] We compare reinforcement, clearer specifications and a warranty, with the trade-offs visible. [Show scene 5.] And we state the success measure and the result that would reverse our recommendation.

For a Kearney PERLab engagement, the same workflow can connect approved client feedback to a renewal experiment and a cost guardrail. Today the backpack is synthetic and the Kearney view uses public descriptions. A real pilot needs approved customer evidence, product economics and a controlled test. We help teams go from ‘people are complaining’ to ‘here is the next decision worth testing.’”

## Judge questions and answers

| Likely question | Answer to give |
| --- | --- |
| What is actually new here? | “The useful unit is a decision with traceable evidence, an opposing segment and a reversal rule. Sentiment charts and review summaries exist; our demo shows how to turn disagreement into a product experiment.” Do not claim this is the first product to do so. |
| Is the backpack data real? | “No. All three passages and their need tags in the guided story are fictional and pre-coded. We label them that way. We haven't measured any sales lift.” |
| Does AI extract those needs live? | “Not in that guided backpack story. Its tags are pre-coded. The broader Kearney research flow has optional server-side AI, subject to configuration and provider availability, and its output still needs human review.” |
| Why not just use ChatGPT or a review dashboard? | “A generic summary can suggest themes. Our workflow keeps source type and exact supporting passages visible, makes missing or conflicting evidence explicit, and requires a test and reversal condition. The comparison should be evaluated with real users; we haven't proven time savings yet.” |
| How do you know this would increase sales? | “We don't yet. A preference study can prioritize concepts, but a sales-lift claim needs a controlled launch with comparable groups, defined eligibility and a measurement window. We would track conversion, returns and margin together.” |
| What would you measure in the pilot? | “Time to a defensible decision, unsupported claim rate, reviewer agreement on extracted needs, and whether the proposed test has a measurable outcome. In the later launch: conversion, returns, cost and contribution margin by variant.” |
| Where do non-buyers come from? | “They need their own channel: consideration surveys, abandoned-cart research, product questions or interviews, with consent and sampling documented. Reviews alone cannot recover the people who never purchased.” |
| How do you avoid biased reviews? | “We show source and segment, record what is missing, and avoid treating mention counts as market prevalence. A representative sample or an experiment is needed before generalizing.” |
| How do you handle contradictory feedback? | “We preserve the contradiction. Heavy-load and light-load users might need different designs or different positioning. The experiment tests the trade-off by segment.” |
| Why Kearney/PERLab specifically? | “PERLab works on product value, customer needs and cost or quality trade-offs. SignalScope is a proposed evidence-to-decision workbench for that work, not an official Kearney product or a connection to its private systems.” |
| Can this work for any product? | “The evidence and decision structure is reusable, but each category needs its own product attributes, segment definitions, outcomes and constraints. We would start with one product family, not claim universal coverage.” |
| What is production ready? | “The synthetic demo and a single-team pilot workflow exist. Enterprise use still needs approved data access, identity and audit controls, retention policies, reliable integrations and evaluation on real cases.” |
| What if the data or the AI is wrong? | “Traceability lets the reviewer challenge the exact source and reject a bad interpretation. Missing evidence stays unknown. The product owner should approve the experiment before any real-world change.” |
| What would Kearney need to give you for a real pilot? | “A sponsor, one product family with SKU and region, a permitted redacted evidence export, cost and returns definitions, an accountable reviewer and a product owner. Credentials and raw private data belong in an approved environment.” |

## Presenter checklist

- Start the demo on the fictional backpack and open `/opportunities` before judging begins. Click through the five scenes once.
- Keep `/kearney` in another tab. Use its public offering description; only show a generated comparison or roadmap if a completed, source-backed report is actually present.
- Say “fictional,” “hypothesis,” and “test” plainly. Never claim that the displayed example proved revenue growth.
- If the network or AI fails, finish with the local five-scene story and explain the intended pilot. The core pitch does not depend on a model call.
